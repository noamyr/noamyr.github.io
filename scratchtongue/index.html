Technical notes

Method. Common contractions and reduced forms are expanded, then the input is tokenized into words and punctuation. Articles and selected grammatical function words are removed before semantic lookup. Exact Swadesh matches are normalized directly. With inflection handling on, common irregulars and part-of-speech-specific suffix rules generate candidate lemmas, retained only when present in the corresponding WordNet index; a rule-based context pass orders plausible parts of speech. In WordNet mode, unmatched words are searched by ordered semantic confidence: same-synset synonym; with Interpretative relations on, direct antonym with negation; hypernym; then similar-to, verb-group, directional derivation toward a morphological base, participle, pertainym, attribute, entailment, cause, and meronym/holonym links. Disabling interpretative relations leaves synonym and hypernym matching only. Weighted traversal preserves this priority across multi-step paths. Odd antonym parity prefixes not. Maximum distance limits WordNet relation steps. Singular gendered third-person pronouns normalize to they; gender-neutral human terms to person; man and woman are produced only from explicitly gendered source terms. Output is sentence-cased.

Swadesh list. A Swadesh list is “a compilation of tentatively universal concepts for the purposes of lexicostatistics. That is, a Swadesh list is a list of forms and concepts which all languages, without exception, have terms for, such as star, hand, water, kill, sleep, and so forth. The number of such terms is small – a few hundred at most, or possibly less than a hundred. The inclusion or exclusion of many terms is subject to debate among linguists; thus, there are several different lists, and some authors may refer to ‘Swadesh lists.’ The Swadesh list is named after linguist Morris Swadesh.

Translations of a Swadesh list into a set of languages allow for researchers to quantify the interrelatedness of those languages. Swadesh lists are used in lexicostatistics (the quantitative assessment of the genealogical relatedness of languages) and glottochronology (the dating of language divergence). For instance, the terms on a Swadesh list can be compared between two languages (since both languages will have them) to see if they are related and how closely, thus giving useful information that can be further applied to comparison of the languages. (Actual lexicostatistics is quite complicated, and usually sets of languages are compared.)” Wikipedia
